Hi there! My name is Taylor Justason, a wildlife biologist with a passion for large mammal ecology and conservation, human-wildlife coexistence, and protected areas. 

Over the past five years, I’ve contributed professionally in diverse areas of ecology and conservation in academia, government, and NGOs, with roles focusing on large mammal ecology and conservation, human-wildlife coexistence, wildlife trade, and impact assessment. My work has taken me from the jungles of Indonesian Borneo to the Canadian subarctic. In my most recent roles at Parks Canada, I have collaborated on wildlife work such as grizzly bear denning habitat models, supported and monitored compliance of pre- and post-decision impact assessment, and produced species at risk Action Plans in Nahanni and Elk Island National Parks. Earlier roles at The University of British Columbia (UBC) and the Borneo Orangutan Survival Foundation had me helping design and manage studies on the impacts of recreation on wildlife, the trade in wild and captive seahorses, and the success of reintroduced orangutan populations. 

I’m currently taking a break from work with Parks Canada to pursue an MSc in Biodiversity and Conservation at the University of Freiburg. I previously received my BSc in Biology from UBC. While at UBC, I produced a directed studies comparing the use of camera traps vs. aquatic eDNA samping for the purpose of performing terrestrial animal community surveys, completed a cooperative education designation, and was a founding member and later president of the university’s chapter of the Wildlife Society. 

Outside of work, I participate in several professional and outreach efforts. I also dabble in botany, with a particularly keen interest in the biogeography and conservation of rare bald and prairie habitats in western Washington and southwest British Columbia.
